### 1. How do AI tools like ChatGPT decide which businesses to recommend?
- URL: https://www.quora.com/How-do-AI-tools-like-ChatGPT-decide-which-businesses-to-recommend
- Draft:
> For local businesses, it's less of a decision than people think. When someone asks "who's a good roofer in Tampa," ChatGPT (and Gemini, Claude, Perplexity) usually runs a live web search, reads a handful of pages, and summarizes who looks credible for that exact request.
>
> So the businesses that get named tend to have three things going for them:
>
> 1. **The sources agree.** Name, address, phone, hours and service area match across Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing, Apple Maps and industry directories. When sources conflict, the assistant often plays it safe and names someone else.
> 2. **There's specific evidence.** Reviews and web pages that mention the actual service and the town ("fixed our roof leak in Brandon after the storm") match the question far better than "great service, five stars."
> 3. **Other people mention them.** Local news, chamber of commerce listings, supplier "find a pro" pages, best-of lists. Assistants lean on what independent sites say more than on a business's own website.
>
> It isn't a pure quality judgment. The model can't know who does the best work; it knows who the web describes most clearly and consistently for that request. That's frustrating for good businesses with a thin online footprint, but it also means it's fixable.
>
> The quickest way to see it for yourself: ask two or three assistants for your service in your town, phrased a few different ways, and look at which sites they cite for the businesses they do name. Those sites are what they're reading.
>
> Disclosure: I build Alphaa, which does this checking and fixing for local businesses, so I'm biased. Everything above you can do yourself.

### 2. How can local businesses appear in AI search results like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity?
- URL: https://www.quora.com/How-can-local-businesses-appear-in-AI-search-results-like-ChatGPT-Gemini-or-Perplexity
- Draft:
> Start by finding out where you stand, because the fix depends on it. Ask each of them the way a customer would ("emergency plumber near Plano," "best family dentist in Plano for kids") and write down who gets named and which websites get cited.
>
> Then work through this, roughly in order:
>
> 1. **Clean up your listings.** Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect and Yelp, with identical name, address, phone, hours and service area. Yelp matters more than people expect, since its data feeds some AI answers.
> 2. **Don't block the AI crawlers.** Check your robots.txt (or ask whoever built your site) to make sure crawlers like OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot are allowed.
> 3. **Answer real questions on your own site.** A plain page per main service, plus answers to what customers actually ask (cost range, how long it takes, which towns you cover), with the answer in the first sentence.
> 4. **Ask for specific reviews.** "Can you mention what we did and where?" gets you reviews that match real searches.
> 5. **Get mentioned elsewhere.** Chamber listings, local news, supplier directories, local best-of roundups. These are often the sites the assistants cite.
> 6. **Claim your ChatGPT business profile** if it's available for your business type and location. It helps keep your details accurate, though it doesn't guarantee a recommendation.
>
> Re-run your test questions every month or so. Answers shift as the sources change.
>
> Disclosure: I build Alphaa, a tool that automates this for local businesses, so I'm biased. None of it requires a tool.
