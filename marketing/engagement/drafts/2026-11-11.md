### 1. How do I get my business to appear in ChatGPT and Perplexity answers?
- URL: https://www.quora.com/How-do-I-get-my-business-to-appear-in-ChatGPT-and-Perplexity-answers
- Draft:
> There's no form to submit and no fee to pay. Both tools build their answer from what the web already says about you, so the work is making that consistent and specific.
>
> What tends to matter most for a local business:
>
> 1. Your name, address, phone, hours and service area are identical everywhere: Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, your industry directories. Conflicting info is the most common reason an assistant skips a business it clearly knows about.
> 2. Reviews that name the actual service and the town ("replaced our water heater in Plano"), not just "great service." When someone asks for "a plumber in Plano for a water heater," those words are what the assistant matches on.
> 3. Pages on your own site that answer the questions customers really ask (what it costs, how long it takes, do you cover my area), in plain language, with the answer in the first sentence.
> 4. Being mentioned by other people: local news, the chamber of commerce, supplier "find an installer" pages, best-of lists in your city. Assistants trust what others say about you more than what you say about yourself.
> 5. For ChatGPT specifically, as of today (Nov 11, 2026) OpenAI lets businesses claim and verify a profile in ChatGPT through its new Business Hub (beta). It's worth claiming so your details are accurate, though OpenAI is clear that claiming it doesn't guarantee a recommendation.
>
> Then test it. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity the way a customer would ("best emergency plumber in [town]"), a few different ways, and note who they name and which sites they cite. The cited sites are your to-do list.
>
> Disclosure: I build Alphaa, a tool that does this checking and fixing for local businesses, so I'm biased. Everything above you can do yourself.

### 2. How do businesses get found by AI chatbots?
- URL: https://www.quora.com/How-do-businesses-get-found-by-AI-chatbots
- Draft:
> Roughly two ways, and it helps to know which one you're dealing with.
>
> Some answers come from what the model learned in training, which is a snapshot of the web. Others come from a live web search the chatbot runs while answering (ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude with search). For local businesses it's mostly the second, because "who's a good dentist near me" needs current info.
>
> In that live search, the chatbot reads a handful of pages (your site, your Google and Yelp listings, review sites, local news, directories) and summarizes who looks credible for the exact request. So a business gets "found" when:
>
> - those sources agree on who you are, where you are and what you do,
> - there's specific evidence for the thing being asked about (reviews and pages that mention "Invisalign" or "tankless water heater install," not just "dental services"), and
> - independent sites mention you, not only your own website.
>
> A quick self-check: ask two or three chatbots for your service in your town, phrased a few different ways, and look at which sites they cite for the businesses they do name. That shows you what they're reading.
>
> Disclosure: I build Alphaa, which automates this for local businesses, so I'm biased. None of the above needs a tool.
